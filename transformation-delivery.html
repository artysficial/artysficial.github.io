How I run a workstream

Own it from diagnosis to handover.

The rhythm I use as Test Lead on a central bank rollout, and would bring to any transformation workstream.

  1. 1 · Diagnose

    Start from the data and the people doing the work: where time, quality or money is lost, and why.

  2. 2 · Plan

    Agree outcomes, milestones, KPIs and entry/exit criteria; keep risks and issues in a RAID log from day one.

  3. 3 · Work with the frontline

    Run workshops with client and frontline teams to shape the solution with them, not for them.

  4. 4 · Track and escalate

    A weekly dashboard of progress and risks; escalate early, with options rather than problems.

  5. 5 · Embed

    Coach the client team until they run the new way of working without us.

Worked diagnostic

One question, answered with data.

A procurement diagnostic from my portfolio case study (simulated bank, seeded SAP-shaped data): 144 purchase orders, 136 invoices, Rp 60.5 bn committed.

The question

Why are only 72% of suppliers paid on time, with late payments running 7.9 days late on average?

Issue tree: where could the delay come from?

  • Invoices blocked at three-way matchPrice variance, missing goods receipt or quantity mismatch stop payment until resolved.95% match first time; 7 open match exceptions
  • Buying outside the processPurchases made before a PO exists need a retrospective PO and approval.2 purchases before PO; 1 split order
  • Invoice and vendor qualityDuplicates and errors are caught late and held back.1 duplicate invoice worth Rp 488 m
  • Slow upstream cycleRequests that take long to become orders push everything downstream.PR to PO: 5.4 days average, 8 at P90

Recommendation

  1. Block the duplicate invoice before the next payment run, so Rp 488 m is not paid twice.
  2. Clear the 7 match exceptions weekly in a short buyer and accounts-payable huddle; most need a goods receipt posted or a price confirmed.
  3. Re-brief requesters on PO-first buying and review the split order against the approval threshold.
  4. Track on-time payment on the dashboard against the 80% target, so the improvement is visible each week.

The so-what for the CFO

Spend is under control at 86% of budget; the cash and supplier risk sits in a small number of exceptions that a weekly routine can clear.
