Quality engineering for Central Bank Digital Currency

What changes when the client is a central bank: risk-based test strategy, entry and exit criteria, device and card coverage, and reporting that supports a go/no-go call.

Rahmadani Tyas Ningrum Test Lead & Senior Automation SDET

When the client is a central bank

A Central Bank Digital Currency is money issued directly by a central bank in digital form. Testing it is not like testing an ordinary fintech app. The issuer is the institution responsible for the stability of the currency itself, the users include people who may never have held a bank account, and a defect can undermine public trust in money, not just in a product.

I have tested CBDC solutions alongside international central banks, including the Bank of Canada and the Bank of Israel, and now lead testing for a CBDC deployment for a client in Malawi. These are the principles that have held up across all of them.

Start from risk, not from screens

A CBDC platform typically spans an issuance and ledger core, mobile and web wallets, back-office portals for operators and support staff, and often smart cards for offline or low-connectivity payments. It is tempting to plan testing screen by screen. A stronger strategy starts from the risks the central bank cares about most:

  • Monetary integrity: money is never created, destroyed or duplicated outside authorised issuance and redemption, and every ledger movement reconciles.
  • Limits and tiers: holding and transaction limits by KYC tier are enforced everywhere, including edge cases at exactly the limit.
  • Offline payments: card-based and offline transactions cannot be double-spent and reconcile correctly once back online.
  • Inclusion: the experience works on low-end devices, on poor networks and for first-time users.
  • Operational control: operator and support actions are authorised, auditable and reversible where policy allows.

Each risk maps to test scenarios, and the depth of testing follows the size of the risk. That mapping is also what I walk the client through, because it shows them where effort is going and why.

Entry and exit criteria everyone signs up to

Clear criteria remove arguments at the end of a cycle by settling them at the start. A representative set:

GateCriteria
EntryBuild deployed to the test environment with release notes; smoke suite passing; test data and devices ready; requirements for the scope baselined.
ExitAll planned tests executed; no open critical or high defects without an agreed workaround; reconciliation checks clean; regression suite passing; residual risks documented and accepted by the client.

Covering mobile, web and cards

Wallet features have to behave the same on iOS, Android and web, but the right testing approach differs by platform. In my work, web flows are automated, iOS and parts of Android are tested manually, and anything that needs a physical smart card tapped against a phone is tested by hand on real devices. Pretending those card interactions can be fully simulated hides exactly the defects that matter in the field.

A small, deliberate device matrix (recent and older OS versions, a low-memory Android device, a range of screen sizes) catches far more than a long list of near-identical phones.

Leading the test team

  • One plan, shared ownership. Mobile and web testers work from the same test plan and defect conventions, so results are comparable.
  • Daily triage. Short, regular defect triage with development keeps severity honest and stops the backlog from surprising anyone.
  • Traceability. Every test links back to a requirement, so coverage gaps are visible before the client finds them.
  • Mentoring. Newer testers pair on exploratory sessions in the riskiest areas; that is where judgement is learnt.

Speaking the client's language

Central bank stakeholders do not need pass rates; they need to know whether the system is safe to put in front of the public, and what remains uncertain. As the primary QA contact, I report quality in terms of the risks above: which are covered, which defects affect them, and what is still open. The detail is in the appendix for anyone who wants it.

The test lead's job is to give the client an honest, evidence-based basis for a go/no-go decision. The structure I use for that report is in Release-readiness reporting clients can act on.
